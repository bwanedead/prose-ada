Jonas looked up when she came in. "So what was it?"

She looked at him. It took her a moment to understand that he was asking her something.

"What time is it?"

"What?"

"What time is it."

He glanced at the clock. "Twenty oh-five. Why?"

She didn't answer. He kept looking at her.

"Whoa. Hey."

"I'm fine."

"You're not. Sit down." He got up and took the flashlight out of her hand, which she hadn't realized she was still holding. "You're white. Did you hit your head up there?"

"No."

"Are you going to throw up?"

She thought about it honestly. "I don't think so."

He pushed the chair with the cracked armrest toward her and she sat. He stood there looking at her, then picked up the phone on the desk. She heard him tell Holloway that Ada wasn't feeling well, that he had the sequence, that it was fine. He listened for a while and said, "Yeah. No, I've got it."

He hung up. "Go home."

"I can do the—"

"You can't. I've got it. It's just her fields, I can run her fields." He handed her her bag. "Go home and sleep. Text me when you get there."

She wanted to tell him. She could feel it sitting right there, all of it, and she opened her mouth, and she couldn't find the beginning. Every way she started it in her head sounded like a woman who hadn't slept. She closed her mouth.

"Okay," she said.

The drive home was the same road, all of it dark now. She kept both hands on the wheel and didn't look up once. At the top of the hill by the pond she slowed, and then she didn't stop, and then she was past it.

The apartment was the way she'd left it. She didn't turn on the lamp. She went straight through to the bedroom and fell onto the bed in her jacket, and her body gave up the rest of the way, sinking into the mattress and into sleep almost before she had stopped moving.
