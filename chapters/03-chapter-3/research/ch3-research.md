# Prose Ada Ch3 research notes

## Hopi Prophecy Rock (near Oraibi / Hotevilla, Third Mesa area)
- Petroglyph near Oraibi, AZ. Popular "Hopi Life Plan" reading: a figure (Creator/Maasaw) at left, then two parallel horizontal paths. The upper path is materialism/the white man's way and ends in destruction/disarray (a zigzag). The lower path leads to growing corn and old age (a figure with a cane). A vertical connecting line marks a last chance to switch paths. Sources: https://native-americans.com/prophecy-rock/ ; https://nwtrcc.org/2019/10/17/converging-and-choosing-our-path/
- CONTESTED. The "Life Plan" reading was promoted by the Traditionalist movement (Thomas Banyacya, Hotevilla elders) and taken up by New Age groups (LaVan Martineau's "translation").
  - The earliest record is John Wesley Powell, 1875. His informant Tal-ti said the ancestors made it to record a Spanish attack on the town.
  - The Hopi Tribal Council position is described as historical. Armin Geertz, *The Invention of Prophecy* (UC Press), treats it as a politically contested "boundary object."
  - Sources: https://janeway.uncpress.org/capstone/article/3022/galley/3093/download/ ; https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1525/9780520311084-010/html
- Usage in the draft: Ada meets the two-paths reading first and almost immediately the dispute. She notes it as a claim, not a fact.

## Hopi emergence / worlds
- Clan versions vary. All describe people emerging from lower worlds to the present (fourth) world through an opening, by a cane/reed or pine tree. The sipapu in the kiva floor symbolizes the opening. Source: Lockett, *The Unwritten Literature of the Hopi* (1933), summarizing A.M. Stephen/Mindeleff, https://www.gutenberg.org/files/15888/15888-h/15888-h.htm
- Penn Museum: emergence from Sipaapuni into the Fourth Way of Life; migration covenant with Maasaw; petroglyphs as "footprints" of clan migration. https://www.penn.museum/sites/expedition/ang-kuktota/

## Spirals / mazes
- Mesa Verde, Petroglyph Point Trail panel. According to one Hopi elder (four Hopi men visited in 1942), the boxy spiral is likely the sipapu, the emergence place, and nearby figures mark clans separating. The spiral where the line ends is either the end of migration at Mesa Verde or the prophesied end at the Hopi villages. NPS says interpretations change over time. https://www.nps.gov/meve/learn/education/artifactgallery_petroglyph.htm ; McAllister 1981 https://digitalcommons.sacredheart.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1143&context=shureview
- Fajada Butte "Sun Dagger," Chaco Canyon. It is Chacoan / Ancestral Puebloan, NOT specifically Hopi.
  - Three slabs cast light onto two spiral petroglyphs. The large spiral is about 34 × 41 cm with about 9.5 turns. At summer solstice a dagger bisects it; at winter solstice two daggers bracket it; at the equinoxes a shaft hits the smaller spiral.
  - Anna Sofaer rediscovered it in 1977 (Science 1979).
  - Closed to the public since 1982. The slabs shifted by 1989, so the daggers no longer center.
  - Sources: https://www2.hao.ucar.edu/education/prehistoric-southwest/sun-dagger ; https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.206.4416.283 ; https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/archeoastronomy-in-stone.htm
- Tapu'at ("Mother and Child") maze.
  - The meaning "emergence / rebirth from one world to the next" comes chiefly from Frank Waters, *Book of the Hopi* (1963). Scholars caution that Waters is literary and esoteric rather than ethnographic.
  - Petroglyph examples are reported south of Oraibi.
  - Sources: https://heartfeltdolls.weebly.com/hopi-labyrinth.html ; http://www.scielo.org.mx/scielo.php?pid=S0185-16592009000200011&script=sci_arttext
  - Not used in Ch3 (saved for later).
- Attribution rule: Fajada = Chacoan/Ancestral Puebloan. Mesa Verde = Ancestral Puebloan glyph with a modern Hopi interpretation. Prophecy Rock and Tapu'at = Hopi (contested or secondhand readings).

## Jacket / object (plausible, invented)
- A national park souvenir embroidered patch ("MESA VERDE" with a petroglyph-style spiral) sewn on the back of a canvas work jacket worn by a customer. Park gift-shop patches are ubiquitous. The specific design is invented, so it shouldn't be described as an official design. Respectful: it reads as a tourist souvenir, and she doesn't treat it as a sacred object.

## Potter trail circa 2010-2020 (invented, plausible)
- Literature database author search: a handful of records, 1964-1972, small journals. Common surname, so there's noise.
- Course catalog scans (many universities digitized old catalogs to Internet Archive / HathiTrust) list "Potter, E., Asst. Prof." in a physics department, about 1969-72, then gone.
- Alumni magazine class notes (scanned): a death notice for 1974, no cause, few words.
- The Wayback Machine has nothing (pre-web).
